How Bias Enters Data Systems

Historical Bias

Past inequality becomes part of the data.

Example: If a company historically hired mostly men for technical roles, a hiring algorithm may learn that male applicants are more successful candidates.

Measurement Bias

The data collected may not accurately represent reality.

Example: Arrest records do not simply measure crime. They also reflect policing patterns, neighborhood surveillance, and enforcement priorities.

Proxy Bias

A system may avoid using sensitive categories directly but still rely on related signals.

Example: Even if race is not included in a credit algorithm, ZIP code, income, or education history may act as proxies for race or class.

Feedback Loops

Algorithmic decisions can create new data that reinforces the original system.

Example: If predictive policing sends more officers to one neighborhood, more arrests may be recorded there, making the algorithm believe the area is even higher risk.
