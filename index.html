Signals Are Not Neutral

Why Behavioral Data Can Produce Unequal Outcomes

Watch Time

Measures: How long a user stays with content.

Why platforms use it: It predicts attention and retention.

Equity risk: Outrage and fear can also keep people watching.

Likes

Measures: Lightweight positive feedback.

Why platforms use it: Fast engagement signal.

Equity risk: Can favor already-visible groups and styles.

Shares

Measures: Propagation potential.

Why platforms use it: Strong predictor of virality.

Equity risk: Sensational content can spread disproportionately.

Comments

Measures: Depth of interaction.

Why platforms use it: Indicates high engagement.

Equity risk: Conflict-heavy threads can dominate ranking.

Follows

Measures: Network preference and trust.

Why platforms use it: Supports personalization quality.

Equity risk: Reinforces network centrality and incumbency.

Search History

Measures: Prior topics and intent.

Why platforms use it: Improves recommendation relevance.

Equity risk: Can lock users into narrow content paths.

Location / Language

Measures: Regional and linguistic context.

Why platforms use it: Improves local matching.

Equity risk: Can become proxies for social inequality.

Device / Account Settings

Measures: Technical and account-level metadata.

Why platforms use it: Performance and compatibility.

Equity risk: May encode infrastructure and access gaps.

Negative Feedback

Measures: "Not interested" and report behavior.

Why platforms use it: Helps suppress low-value content.

Equity risk: Reporting patterns can be socially uneven.

Where Algorithmic Harms Appear

Where Algorithmic Harms Appear

Misinformation Amplification

What happens: High-arousal false claims gain rapid exposure.

Why signals matter: Shares and watch time can reward controversy.

Why equity matters: Vulnerable communities face uneven harm.

Takeaway: Virality control is a fairness issue.

Political Polarization

What happens: Users are repeatedly exposed to ideologically aligned streams.

Why signals matter: Prior engagement narrows future recommendations.

Why equity matters: Public discourse becomes less shared.

Takeaway: Exposure design shapes civic outcomes.

Creator Visibility Inequality

What happens: Established creators compound reach over time.

Why signals matter: Past exposure drives future ranking score.

Why equity matters: New and marginalized creators struggle to break in.

Takeaway: Ranking can reproduce opportunity gaps.

Moderation and Demotion Bias

What happens: Some communities perceive uneven moderation and downranking.

Why signals matter: Risk labels and trust scores alter distribution.

Why equity matters: Reduced visibility changes participation power.

Takeaway: Moderation and ranking are linked fairness systems.

How Can Feeds Be Made Fairer?

Three Paths to Better Outcomes

Technical Fixes

  • Fairness constraints in ranking objectives
  • Diversity re-ranking
  • Risk penalties for harmful content
  • Exposure audits across groups

Product Design Fixes

  • "Why am I seeing this?" explanations
  • Reset recommendation controls
  • Chronological mode
  • Topic-level personalization settings

Policy Fixes

  • Transparency reporting requirements
  • Independent algorithm audits
  • Researcher access to platform data
  • Appeal and correction systems
What You Should Remember

Key Takeaways

Algorithms do not simply reflect user preference; they shape it.
Engagement is measurable, but not always meaningful.
Signals can become proxies for social inequality.
Visibility is a form of power.
Fairer feeds require technical, design, and policy changes.
