Project Check-In Answer

Title: Algorithmic Signals and Social Inequality: How Data Shapes Opportunity in the Digital Age

Brief overview: My final project is a web-based educational resource about how algorithms use data signals to make decisions and how those decisions can affect equity. The site explains what signals are, how they are used in algorithmic systems, and why signals are not always neutral. It includes examples from hiring algorithms, credit scoring, predictive policing, and social media platforms. It also explains how bias can enter datasets through historical inequality, measurement problems, proxy variables, and feedback loops. The final resource includes case studies, visual explanations, and an interactive example showing how different signals can affect a fictional algorithmic decision.

Outside readings/resources: The most relevant outside resources include Cathy O'Neil's Weapons of Math Destruction, Safiya Umoja Noble's Algorithms of Oppression, ProPublica's "Machine Bias" investigation, AI Now Institute reports on algorithmic accountability, and MIT Technology Review articles on AI fairness.

Created for a project on signals, data, and equity.

This resource is intended for educational use.
