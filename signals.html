What Are Signals in Data Systems?

In data systems, a signal is any piece of information that helps a system make a prediction or decision.

Signal Cards

Signal: Clicks

What it shows: Immediate interaction.

Risk: Rewards attention spikes.

Signal: Search history

What it shows: Interests and intent.

Risk: Reveals sensitive patterns.

Signal: Location data

What it shows: Place-based behavior.

Risk: Can proxy class or race.

Signal: Purchase history

What it shows: Spending and access.

Risk: Penalizes limited resources.

Signal: School records

What it shows: Institutional pathway.

Risk: Encodes unequal opportunities.

Signal: Employment history

What it shows: Career sequence.

Risk: Gaps can be misread.

Signal: Credit history

What it shows: Prior repayment behavior.

Risk: Excludes people with thin files.

Signal: Arrest records

What it shows: Law-enforcement data traces.

Risk: Reflects policing patterns.

Signal: Social media engagement

What it shows: Likes, shares, and comments.

Risk: Amplifies unequal visibility.

Algorithms rarely understand people directly. Instead, they interpret signals. For example, a hiring algorithm may not know whether someone will be a good employee, but it may use signals such as education, previous job titles, writing style, or employment gaps to make a prediction.

The problem is that signals are not neutral. A signal can reflect historical inequality. For example, if past hiring data favored certain groups, an algorithm trained on that data may learn to favor similar applicants in the future.
